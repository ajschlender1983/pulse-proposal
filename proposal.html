Proposal · working draft

Sixty days to sold out.

Aug 1 – Oct 1 · Prepared by Adam Schlender · numbers cited inline · July 2026

01Executive thesis

For a total campaign cost of $45,000 over 60 days — about six weeks of the current ad budget — we test whether this distribution model clears the 1,500 remaining rings and brings the cost of acquiring a customer down to $100. If it works, Pulse has earned the right to raise money for inventory against proven demand. If it doesn't, we've spent less than two months of ad spend to find out — and we keep every asset the campaign built.

The comparison that matters. Every dollar below is judged against one alternative: putting the same $45,000 into Meta ads at today's return of $1.30 in sales per $1 spent3. That baseline produces roughly 255 rings sold and a net loss of about $7,800 after product costs. The campaign has to beat that number — not a slide-deck fantasy, that number.

Three scenarios, priced honestly. Variable costs (the $100-off codes and creator commissions) only occur when a ring actually sells — so the downside is capped at the $45,000 of fixed cost, and the upside scales with sales.

02The model

This is not an event sponsorship plus some ads. It's a repeatable sequence:

1 · Anchor

An event that sells

A real cultural moment (the eclipse, a dance floor, a salon) motivates pre-orders *before* the moment — "get the ring in time for it."

2 · Amplify

Partners carry it

Unify's 200K list + 2.8M social1, seven hosts' audiences, whitelisted ads from their handles — all performance-tracked.

3 · Sell through

The batch clears

A real, visible count of 1,500 rings. When it's gone, it's gone until the next production run. Honest scarcity, live number.

4 · Raise

Fund inventory, not hope

The next raise buys production against demonstrated sell-through — a batch-financing conversation, not a growth-hope conversation.

So what · for ElliotThe $45K isn't a marketing gamble — it's the cost of earning the right to fundraise for inventory with a straight face.
