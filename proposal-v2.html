The thesis.

Iceland is confirmed. For a total campaign cost of $45,000 over 60 days — everything included: Iceland, the events, the retainer, and the $16K of ad spend — we test whether this distribution model clears the 1,500 remaining rings and brings the cost of acquiring a customer down to $100. If it works, Pulse has earned the right to raise money for inventory against proven demand. If it doesn't, we spent about six weeks of the current ad budget to find out — and we keep every asset the campaign built.

The story.

On August 12, for the first time since 1954, the sky over Iceland goes dark in the middle of the day. Two thousand people will be seated in a mandala on the Snæfellsnes Peninsula — a ceremony Unify has spent a year building. At the moment of totality, everyone sets an intention.

Then the moment passes and Monday comes back. That's the exact problem Pulse solves: the ring is the physical cue that carries the intention past the moment.

We are confirmed as a partner, integrated into the primary ceremony. Adam is on the ground with intimate access to the 500 VIPs — a room that includes investors, partners, and ambassadors, and is worth more than any one of those labels. And we sell there. From the Ceremony Garden we announce the integration event — the gathering that carries the totality intention into daily practice — and rings are available on-site, promoted as the way to carry the moment home. Seven weeks later, the batch of 1,500 is gone.

The brand's film-strip device — the same moment, held at the speed of being here. This grammar runs through every campaign surface.

Every participant gets a personal map — the same design we built for guided first-month journeys on SoundBed, adapted to seven days. They set one intention at signup. It sits at the top of their map all week.

Each day holds the host's short piece, a morning question, and an evening check-in. Days open at 7am and close at 10pm — outside the window, the map rests. No feed, no streaks, no doomscrolling. The product's philosophy, embodied by the campaign that sells it.

At signup, participants tell us if they own a ring. If they don't: a gentle note, once — "the week is even better with one, WOW100 takes $100 off." That's the whole pitch. The week itself does the convincing.
